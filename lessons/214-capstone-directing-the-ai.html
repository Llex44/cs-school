Part 1 of 5 · Stage 1

Write the spec

A spec (or brief) tells the AI what to build, for whom, and what not to build. Everything you leave out, the AI guesses. Its guesses tend to be bigger, more generic and more expensive than what you need.

YouBuild an app for my friend Dana's dog-walking business.
AI assistantDone! Meet DogSpace: a social network for dogs with profiles, a photo feed, chat, a shop and live GPS walk tracking.
YouDana just wants people to book walks…

Your task

  1. Send the one-line prompt as it is, and see what the AI builds.
  2. Pick the strongest line for all five parts of the brief.
  3. Send the full brief and compare the AI's new draft.

Under the hood: "done means…" optional

Strong specs end with acceptance criteria: short, checkable sentences that say when the work is finished. For example: "An owner can book and pay for a walk on a phone in under a minute." "Two owners can't book the same slot."

They're useful twice. You can check the AI's work against them, and the AI can turn each one into a test (course 208).

Review the AI's plan

Good AI tools propose a plan before writing code: the parts, the tools, and how they connect. Catching a bad choice here costs one sentence. Catching it after launch costs weeks. You don't need to know every tool. You need to spot the choices that don't fit.
