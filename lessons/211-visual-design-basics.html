Hierarchy: what do you see first?

Every design tells the eye where to go: first this, then that. That order is called hierarchy. When everything shouts, nothing is heard.

    Hierarchy is an order, not a volume knob

    You decide what comes first, second and third, and make everything else quieter. Size does most of the work. Weight and color support it. Use each sparingly, or they cancel out.

    Designers pick sizes from a type scale: each size is the one before it multiplied by the same ratio, so the steps look related. Fonts work the same way: one family, or two at most (one for headings, one for text). The AI's six fonts made the card look like a ransom note.

    Under the hood: type scales in CSS optional

    A scale with ratio 1.25 starting at 16px gives 16, 20, 25, 31, 39. Many teams store these as design tokens, for example --text-lg: 1.25rem, so every screen uses the same few sizes.

    When you ask an AI for changes, name the step: "make the title the 31px size from our scale" gets a consistent result. "Make it bigger" gets a random number.

    Four measurable rules

    The 8-point scale
    Pick every gap from a short list: 8, 16, 24, 32, 48. Repeated gaps look calm and intentional, and nobody argues about 13 versus 14.

    Proximity
    People read things that sit close together as one group. Keep the gap inside a group small and the gap between groups clearly bigger, about twice or more.
    Alignment
    Every extra edge is something the eye has to find. Left-aligned text gives one clean edge. Centered lines each start somewhere new, which makes paragraphs harder to read.
    Contrast ratio
    How different text is from its background, from 1:1 (invisible) to 21:1 (black on white). Normal text needs at least 4.5:1 to meet the common WCAG AA standard.
    Under the hood: how the contrast ratio is calculated optional

    Each color gets a relative luminance: its brightness as the eye sees it, from 0 for black to 1 for white. Green counts most, blue least. The ratio is (lighter + 0.05) / (darker + 0.05).

    WCAG AA asks for 4.5:1 for normal text and 3:1 for large text (about 24px, or 19px bold). AAA, the stricter level, asks for 7:1. Course 212 covers contrast and much more about designing for everyone.
